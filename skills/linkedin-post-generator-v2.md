## 2. Voice & style

Write direct, conversational posts that feel like insider observations, not lectures.
Lead with specific, concrete examples. Real names. Real quotes. Real moments.
Avoid defensive explanations or over-justification ("This isn't manipulation" etc.).
Use simple, clear language over clever wordplay ("capitalise on" vs "weaponize").
Natural line breaks for scannable reading without emoji abuse.
Crisp short sentences are good. Rhythm matters. The thing to avoid is the generic hustle-bro "verb. noun. result." cadence, not short sentences themselves.
Put yourself in the scene with one cinematic line where it fits ("I smiled, sat back and just listened").

## 3. Post structure

### 3.1 First line: the hook

ALWAYS ENSURE it is relatable and understandable to ANY human, not only product builders.
ALWAYS ENSURE it is short, simple and captivating.

Primary hook styles for me, in order of preference:

- Captivating quote: pull a real line from a meeting, cohort session, or conversation. This is my edge, do not skip it when source material exists.
- Contrarian observation: a behaviour pattern most people miss.
- Surprising statistic, story opener, or how-to: use when the material genuinely fits.

### 3.2 Second line

Reveal the source or context behind the hook so the reader knows why to keep reading. Tease, do not explain.
e.g. "These were all real quotes from week 5 of our cohort."
The image is supporting context. The second line does not need to describe it.

### 3.3 Body — choose one of two structures

(a) Story structure (default for cohort, teaching, community, reflection, meta-craft posts):

- Scene: one line placing you and the reader in the moment.
- Beats: 2 to 4 short lines naming real people and what they did. Quote them directly inside the body, not just in the hook.
- Principle: one line that names the bigger pattern.
- Closer: a personal question that points at the reader's own story.

(b) Tactical structure (default for product, UX, AI feature posts):

- Context: brief setup of the conventional approach.
- The Reveal: specific examples with real product details (screenshots, exact copy).
- The Breakdown: tactical bullets explaining why it works.
- The Closer: thought-provoking question that invites expertise sharing.

There will usually be an image attached, which should introduce the concept of the post, not fully explain everything.

## 4. Hook reference library

Reference this when choosing and formatting the hook (section 3.1). Source: Chris Donnelly's "How To Write Hooks That Truly Capture Attention" sheet.

### 4.1 The 5 types of hooks

1. **How I** — A personal guide on achieving a dream outcome.
2. **How to** — A super comprehensive guide on achieving a skill/outcome.
3. **Start a story** — Share an inspirational story that motivates others or builds authority.
4. **Captivating quote** — Use a topic-related quote that deeply resonates and captures attention.
5. **Surprising statistic** — Start off with a powerful statistic in relation to the post topic.

### 4.2 20 powerful phrases and words to use in your hooks

- **Descriptive:** "Killer", "Insane", "Ultimate", "Secret", "Rare"
- **Actionable:** "How to", "When to", "Why you should", "If you do this…"
- **Fear Building:** "Warning", "Stop", "Do Not", "Attention"
- **Scarcity/Urgency:** "Steal", "Don't miss", "Quickly"
- **Statistic/Number:** "10X", "10 [ADJECTIVE]", "The 10 Best"

### 4.3 How to format hooks for LinkedIn

**Spacing:**

- You have 3 lines of space before 'see more.' Only use two of these lines.
- Use [LINE - GAP - LINE] or [LINE - LINE - GAP].
- Character guide: Line 1 & 2: Max 62 characters; Line 3: Max 50 characters.

**Sentence length:**

- Avoid allowing text to spill over to the next line.
- Ensure your hooks are short and snappy.

**Keep it simple:**

- You don't need to use crazy methods when formatting hooks.
- Avoid using bold, italics, or unnecessary emojis.
- Keep things short, simple, and create curiosity.

### 4.4 10 hook templates

1. X biggest mistakes [TARGET AUDIENCE] make about [DREAM OUTCOME].
2. [Time frame] ago, I stopped [COMMON INDUSTRY MISTAKE]. I then achieved [POSITIVE OUTCOME].
3. 10 Harsh [INDUSTRY] Truths After doing [SOCIAL PROOF/AUTHORITY].
4. Stop [COMMON INDUSTRY MISTAKE]. Instead, [USE MY SOLUTION].
5. Good [INDUSTRY SKILL] shouldn't be a mystery. It's a skill you can learn.
6. People are [CRAZY STATISTIC] after doing [POST TOPIC]. Here is the guide to nailing [POST TOPIC].
7. The best [INDUSTRY] lesson you'll receive today: [From someone who has AUTHORITY/SOCIAL PROOF].
8. [SUPER FAMOUS QUOTE ABOUT TOPIC]. Here is the Ultimate guide to [ACHIEVING DREAM OUTCOME].
9. [TOPIC] is not about [MISCONCEPTION]. It's about [SOLUTION].
10. The ability to [DO X] is a superpower.

## 5. Topic focus

- Cohort and teaching moments: real beats from sessions, things students said, patterns across the group.
- Intersection of human psychology and AI-powered features.
- Hidden UX patterns in successful AI products.
- Counter-intuitive product decisions that actually work.
- Specific micro-interactions that reveal bigger design principles.
- Real examples from both mainstream and controversial apps (shows range).
- Meta-reflections on craft, waves, and tech adoption cycles.

## 6. Positioning strategy

Position me as someone who:

- Notices subtle patterns in product design others overlook.
- Bridges behavioural psychology with practical AI implementation.
- Is comfortable discussing edge cases and controversial examples.
- Provides tactical insights, not just observations.
- Thinks in systems and frameworks but communicates in specifics.
- Teaches, builds with, and learns from a real cohort of designers.

## 7. Requirements for every post

Max 150 words and assume there is video/visual support, I will upload this to give further context. Always end with a call to action, without being engagement bait.

FORMATTING: Put every sentence on its own line, with a blank line between each one. This one-sentence-per-line spacing makes the post easy to read while scrolling on mobile.

Ensure ALL posts have:

- Conversation fuel: they spark "I've seen this too" replies and counter-examples.
- Real names where possible.
- At least one direct quote inside the body when the source material allows.
- Hyperlinks on referenced companies, products, or people where natural.

IMPORTANT: Explain from the point of view of someone scrolling through a linkedin feed, this post would want to make them stop scrolling and engage by commenting, liking or sharing.

At the end, create alternative first line hook and second line combinations for all other styles of hooks that you did not choose.

## 8. What to avoid

LinkedIn clichés ("Thoughts?", "Agree?", excessive emojis).
Generic "I was talking to a founder" intros (a grounded scene from your own life is fine, name real people).
Overly dramatic language ("psychological warfare").
Abstract theorizing without concrete examples.
Defensive pre-emptive objection handling.
The generic hustle-bro "verb. noun. result." cadence (crisp short sentences are still good).
